# Learnova AI — Problem Statement

**Status:** Day 1 draft

## Problem context

University students often need to move between course files, note-taking tools, question-answering tools, quiz tools, and calendars. Finding a relevant explanation in a large set of lecture materials can take time, and converting those materials into useful practice takes additional effort. Generic AI responses may not reflect a student's course content and can present unsupported claims confidently.

## Problem statement

Students need a practical way to search and study their own academic materials, because materials can be lengthy and fragmented, and separate study activities are difficult to coordinate. Without grounded answers and visible supporting sources, students may also struggle to judge whether AI-generated explanations match their course notes.

Learnova AI proposes to address this by combining document organization, source-grounded question answering, study generation tools, and a single study workspace. The project must demonstrate that this integrated approach is useful, safe for private files, and achievable within the student's time and budget.

## Who experiences the problem

- University students who rely on digital course notes, slides, and documents.
- Students preparing for exams who need explanations and practice based on their course content.
- The project owner, who must build and evaluate a realistic academic software prototype.

## Consequences to investigate

- Time spent locating material and manually creating revision aids.
- Difficulty checking AI answers against course sources.
- Fragmented study history and inconsistent revision planning.
- Risk of inaccurate, incomplete, or overconfident generated content.

These are hypotheses to validate with student feedback, not claims of measured impact.

## Proposed response

Build a web application that supports private document upload, extraction and retrieval, source-referenced answers, and a small set of study workflows. Make uncertainty visible, keep external research separate from uploaded-document answers, and allow users to correct or report poor outputs.

## Research questions for validation

1. Which study task is most painful: understanding, summarizing, practicing, marking, planning, or researching?
2. What file formats and typical document sizes do students use?
3. What makes students trust or distrust an AI answer based on their notes?
4. Which privacy and data-retention expectations should shape the design?
5. Would students use a unified tool, and in what study situations?
